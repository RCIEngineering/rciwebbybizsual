The firm

What does RCI Engineering do?

We are RCI Engineering. We design the mechanical, electrical, plumbing and fire protection systems inside buildings and provide commissioning. Architects design the buildings and contractors construct them. We engineer the systems that make them work: air, power, water, fire protection and the controls that tie them together. We were founded in 2005 and work from our office in Davie, Florida.

Who founded RCI Engineering?

We founded RCI Engineering in 2005: Oscar Sebelen, PE, an electrical engineer and Brian Coldwell, PE, a mechanical engineer. Both are registered professional engineers with NCEES records and both are LEED Accredited Professionals. Brian Coldwell is a Certified Commissioning Authority and a Level II Thermographer. Oscar Sebelen is NICET certified. Both still sign the sets.

Where is RCI Engineering located?

Our office is at 5599 S University Drive, Suite 204, Davie, Florida 33328, in Broward County. The phone is 954-680-2690 and the email is info@rcieng.com. Earlier addresses on South University Drive that still appear in some listings are retired.

Where does RCI work?

Most of our work is in South Florida, from Key Biscayne and Miami Beach to Fort Lauderdale, Boca Raton and Palm Beach. Our projects also reach Naples, Marco Island, Bonita Springs, Stuart, Sarasota and Tallahassee. Outside Florida, we designed the systems for two university buildings in True Blue, Grenada. Our professional engineers are licensed in multiple states.

What does "your in-house MEP department" mean?

It is how we describe the way we work with architects, developers and contractors: as the MEP department they would have if they employed one. The same engineers across projects, a questionnaire that opens each job, a basis of design set together and a written update every week, so the architect does not have to manage a consultant from the outside.

Is RCI Engineering a contractor?

No. We are a consulting engineering firm. It designs the mechanical, electrical, plumbing and fire protection systems and issues the drawings a licensed contractor works from. We do not install systems, construct buildings or sell equipment.

Working with RCI

When should an architect bring RCI into a project?

As early as schematic design. Shafts, ceiling heights, equipment locations, roof screens and the electrical service all shape the architecture. Each is easier to place before the plans are fixed. A system decided early can be designed around the architecture. One decided late has to be fitted into it.

How does a project with RCI start?

With a call to our office at 954-680-2690 or an email to info@rcieng.com, ideally with the drawings, the program and the schedule. Once we are engaged, the project opens with an onboarding questionnaire and runs on a written update every week.

What should I send with a first inquiry?

The floor plans and the reflected ceiling plans if they exist, the elevations and sections, the program, the location and the schedule. A sketch is enough to start the conversation. The onboarding questionnaire collects the rest once we are engaged.

What is RCI's onboarding questionnaire?

The document that opens every one of our projects. It records the program, the budget and the deadlines, so that the basis of design can be chosen against all three before anything is drawn.

What is a basis of design?

The record of which systems were chosen for a building and why: the type of air conditioning, the electrical service, the water heating, the fire protection approach, with the assumptions behind each. We set it with the architect at the start, so the drawings that follow answer a decision instead of making one.

How does RCI coordinate with the architect and the other consultants?

From the start. We set the basis of design with the architect. As the set develops, we coordinate routes, equipment and penetrations with the architectural and structural drawings. Our sheets say who coordinates what. On a recent apartment renovation in Miami Beach, the air distribution table tells the contractor to coordinate slot diffuser borders and colors with the architect. The key notes require duct crossings to be checked against the structural drawings. Projects that call for building information modeling are modeled in Revit, as Six13, The Aldridge and The Laramore in Fort Lauderdale were.

How does RCI keep the systems from compromising the design?

By deciding with the architect where each system lives before it is drawn. Equipment goes where the design wants it hidden or shown. Ducts follow the ceiling heights the architect set. Diffusers and grilles are placed on the reflected ceiling plan and their finish is coordinated with the architect. Two of our residences, in Naples and Tallahassee, use variable refrigerant flow (VRF) systems, which carry refrigerant in small pipes instead of pushing air through long runs of large duct.

How are the systems kept within budget?

The budget enters at the start. Our onboarding questionnaire captures the program, the budget and the deadlines. At the basis of design, we choose the systems with the architect and the owner to fit all three before anything is drawn.

What is the weekly update?

A structured, written update while the drawings develop. It tells the architect what moved, what is coordinated and what is waiting on a decision. Every one of our projects runs on it.

Does RCI stay involved during construction?

Yes. During construction administration we answer the contractor's requests for information and review submittals. Replies often come within 48 hours. For two university buildings in True Blue, Grenada, our scope ran from design through construction administration to completion.

Does RCI work on existing buildings?

Yes. On the record: the systems of a 110-room hotel from 1939 in Miami Beach redesigned for a comprehensive renovation, a boutique hotel renovation in Palm Beach with 32 suites and a restaurant, a 3,275 sq ft marina kitchen renovation in Stuart and retail tenant improvements inside existing shell buildings in the Miami Design District.

Does RCI work with developers and contractors as well as architects?

Yes. Architects, developers and contractors all work with us. The record includes projects brought by architecture practices, by a custom-home company and by a construction company.

Drawings, codes and construction

What is in an RCI drawing set?

An RCI permit set covers each discipline in our scope, with floor plans, legends, schedules, details and key notes. Our mechanical floor plans carry an HVAC legend, an air distribution table and key notes that say what to coordinate and with whom. Retail sheets add the contractor's scope of work. Every set filed for permit is signed and sealed by a Florida professional engineer.

What are key notes?

Numbered notes on a sheet that tell the contractor what to coordinate, with whom and under which code section. On our sheets they cite the Florida code section the contractor has to meet, require duct crossings to be checked against the structural drawings and send finish decisions on visible devices to the architect.

Which codes does RCI design to?

We design to the Florida Building Code in force when the permit is filed, with its mechanical, plumbing, fuel gas and energy conservation volumes. Electrical design follows the National Electrical Code as Florida adopts it. Fire protection follows the Florida Fire Prevention Code and the NFPA standards it adopts. In Miami-Dade and Broward counties, the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone adds requirements for equipment exposed to wind. Our key notes cite the code section the contractor has to meet.

Which edition of the Florida Building Code applies?

The edition in force on the day the permit application is filed. Florida adopts a new edition of its building code on a three-year cycle; the 2023 edition took effect at the end of December 2023. Our key notes name the edition each set was designed under, so the contractor and the plan reviewer read the same book.

What is the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone?

The part of Florida, Miami-Dade and Broward counties, where the Florida Building Code applies its strictest wind requirements. For MEP design it means rooftop equipment, curbs and attachments designed and anchored for the wind loads the zone requires, with products approved for use there. Most of our work sits inside it.

Who signs and seals the drawings?

A Florida registered professional engineer at RCI. Every set filed for permit is signed and sealed. Oscar Sebelen, PE, is an electrical engineer and Brian Coldwell, PE, is a mechanical engineer; both are founding principals.

What is a request for information?

A question the contractor sends the design team during construction when something on the drawings needs clarification or a field condition differs from the plan. We answer them during construction administration, often within 48 hours.

What is a submittal?

The contractor's proposed equipment, materials and shop drawings, sent to the engineer for review before purchase and installation. We review submittals against the design intent during construction administration.

Does RCI model in Revit?

Yes, on projects that call for building information modeling. Six13, The Aldridge, The Laramore and Carriage House were modeled in Revit. Other projects are drawn in AutoCAD.

Does RCI prepare the energy calculations the permit requires?

Yes. The compliance forms under the Florida Building Code, Energy Conservation, are part of the permit set, together with the load calculations that size the equipment.

The disciplines

What does MEP mean?

Mechanical, electrical and plumbing: the three engineering disciplines that design the systems inside a building. MEPF adds fire protection. At RCI Engineering we design all four and provide commissioning.

What does mechanical engineering design cover?

The systems that heat, cool, ventilate and dehumidify a building and move smoke out of it in a fire: air handling units, condensing units and heat pumps, variable refrigerant flow systems, rooftop units, ductwork, diffusers and grilles, kitchen exhaust and make-up air, controls and the load calculations that size all of it. Brian Coldwell, PE, signs our mechanical sets.

What is a variable refrigerant flow system?

A VRF system moves refrigerant from one outdoor unit to several indoor units through small pipes. Each indoor unit cools or heats its own zone. It needs far less duct than a conventional system, which is why we use it in residences where ceiling space and quiet matter, as in Naples and Tallahassee and in the Carriage House restaurant and apartments in Palm Beach.

How does South Florida's climate change the mechanical design?

Humidity. A coastal building needs dehumidification as much as cooling, so outdoor air is treated before it enters and equipment is sized for latent load, not only temperature. In Miami-Dade and Broward counties, the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone adds requirements for rooftop equipment, curbs and attachments exposed to wind.

What does electrical engineering design cover?

The service entrance and metering, switchboards and panels, feeders and branch circuits, lighting and lighting controls, receptacles and equipment connections, generators, transfer switches and uninterruptible power, battery storage and the raceways that carry low-voltage systems. Oscar Sebelen, PE, signs our electrical sets.

Does RCI design generators and battery storage?

Yes. Generators, transfer switches and uninterruptible power supply are part of the electrical scope where the program calls for them. A residence in Tallahassee carries battery storage and power walls.

Does RCI design lighting?

Yes, fixtures and controls as part of the electrical set, coordinated with the architect's ceilings and the energy code's lighting power limits. The largest lighting layout on the record is the sports-field lighting for a university campus in True Blue, Grenada: basketball, volleyball, soccer and crossfit.

What does plumbing engineering design cover?

Domestic cold and hot water, water heating, sanitary drainage and venting, storm drainage, fuel gas from the meter or tank to each appliance, grease waste in kitchens, fixtures and the risers that carry all of it through the building.

Does RCI design restaurant kitchens?

Yes. Grease waste and interceptors, gas to the cooking line, water to the fixtures and the drainage a commercial kitchen needs, coordinated with the hood and make-up air on the mechanical sheets. On the record: a marina kitchen in Stuart, a food hall in Miami with 12 or more restaurants and a bar, plus restaurants from Miami Beach to West Palm Beach.

Does RCI design gas systems?

Yes. Propane and natural gas from storage or meter to each appliance, in residences, restaurants and apartment buildings, under the Florida Building Code, Fuel Gas.

Does RCI design fire protection?

Yes. Fire protection is part of our scope on projects such as Elisabetta's and High Dive in West Palm Beach and Carriage House in Palm Beach. Oscar Sebelen, PE, is NICET certified. The scope covers fire sprinkler criteria and layout, fire alarm and detection, the water supply and backflow that feed them and the hazard analysis that sets the design.

What is the difference between RCI's fire protection design and the sprinkler contractor's drawings?

We set the design: the hazard classification, the densities, the zones, the riser, the alarm devices and the sequence. The licensed sprinkler and fire alarm contractors prepare the hydraulic calculations and shop drawings from it. We review them during construction.

Does RCI provide commissioning?

Yes. Our commissioning covers design review, verification of the installed systems and functional testing, through performance optimization and documentation. Brian Coldwell, PE, is a Certified Commissioning Authority.

What is commissioning?

The process that confirms a building's mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems are installed and perform as the design intended. A drawing says what a system should do. Commissioning measures what it does, system by system, before the owner takes the building.

Is commissioning the same as an inspection?

No. An inspection checks a building against the code at one moment. Commissioning verifies that the systems perform as the design intended, through tests the design itself defines. A milestone inspection under Florida Statutes section 553.899 is a structural service performed on condominium and cooperative buildings and it is not commissioning. We are not an inspection firm.

What is a Certified Commissioning Authority?

A credential held by a professional who leads commissioning: writes the plan, verifies the installation, runs the functional tests and reports the results. Brian Coldwell, PE, holds it, together with a Level II Thermographer certification for infrared surveys of electrical and mechanical equipment.

Glossary

MEP
Mechanical, electrical and plumbing. The engineering disciplines that design a building's systems. MEPF adds fire protection.
Basis of design
The record of which systems were chosen for a building and why, set at the start with the architect.
Reflected ceiling plan
The architect's drawing of the ceiling as if seen in a mirror on the floor. Where diffusers, lights, sprinkler heads and devices are coordinated.
Key notes
Numbered notes on a sheet that tell the contractor what to coordinate, with whom and under which code section.
Air distribution table
The schedule on a mechanical sheet that lists each diffuser and grille with its size, airflow and finish notes.
Title block
The corner of a sheet that carries the project, the sheet name and number, the date, the revision log and the engineer's seal.
Sealed set
Drawings signed and sealed by a registered professional engineer, as Florida requires for permit.
Permit set
The drawings filed with the building department for review and permit.
Construction administration
The engineer's work during construction: answering requests for information, reviewing submittals and observing the installation.
RFI
Request for information. A contractor's question to the design team during construction.
Submittal
The contractor's proposed equipment, materials and shop drawings, sent to the engineer for review.
Under air, conditioned, gross
Three ways to measure a building. Under air and conditioned count the cooled space; gross counts everything under roof, including garages and terraces. Our records say which one each number uses.
Core and shell
A building delivered with its structure, envelope and main systems, ready for tenants to fit out the interior.
Tenant improvement
The fit-out of a space inside an existing building for a specific tenant, such as a store or an office.
Podium
A wide lower section of a building, often a garage or retail, with the residential floors set on top of it.
Affordable housing
Apartments rented or sold below market rate under public programs, with their own design and certification rules.
AHU, RTU, VRF
Air handling unit, rooftop unit and variable refrigerant flow: three kinds of equipment that condition a building's air.
Make-up air
The air brought into a kitchen to replace what the hood exhausts.
Grease interceptor
A tank on a kitchen's drain line that holds back grease before it reaches the sewer.
Backflow preventer
A valve assembly that keeps water in a building from flowing back into the public main.
FDC
Fire department connection. The exterior fitting the fire department uses to pump water into a building's sprinkler system.
ATS and UPS
Automatic transfer switch and uninterruptible power supply. The switch moves a building to generator power when the utility fails; the UPS holds selected loads up in the seconds between.
One-line diagram
The electrical sheet that shows the service, the switchboard, feeders, panels and generator as a single line.
BIM and Revit
Building information modeling and the software we use for it: a three-dimensional model of the building and its systems that every discipline works in.
PE
Professional Engineer. A state license to practice engineering and to sign and seal drawings.
NCEES record
A verified record of an engineer's education, examinations and experience, kept by the National Council of Examiners for Engineering and Surveying, used to obtain licenses in other states.
LEED AP
LEED Accredited Professional. A credential in green building practice. Both of our founders hold it.
CxA
Certified Commissioning Authority. The credential of the professional who leads commissioning. Brian Coldwell, PE, holds it.
NICET
The National Institute for Certification in Engineering Technologies. Oscar Sebelen, PE, is NICET certified.
Level II Thermographer
A certification in infrared thermography, used to survey electrical and mechanical equipment for hot spots. Brian Coldwell, PE, holds it, certified by ITC.
Florida Building Code
The state's building code, adopted on a three-year cycle. Its Mechanical, Plumbing, Fuel Gas and Energy Conservation volumes govern most of what we draw.
NEC
The National Electrical Code, NFPA 70, which Florida adopts for electrical design.
Florida Fire Prevention Code
The state's fire code, which adopts NFPA 1 and NFPA 101 and, through them, NFPA 13 and NFPA 72.
HVHZ
The High-Velocity Hurricane Zone: Miami-Dade and Broward counties, where the Florida Building Code applies its strictest wind requirements.
Florida Green Building Certification
A Florida certification for buildings that meet the Florida Green Building Coalition's standard. Six13 in Fort Lauderdale holds it.
Milestone inspection
A structural inspection of condominium and cooperative buildings under Florida Statutes section 553.899. A structural service, not MEP design and not commissioning.
Commissioning
The process that verifies a building's systems are installed and perform as designed: design review, verification of the installation and functional testing.
Studio
At RCI, a team organized around one kind of building: High-end Residential, Commercial or Multifamily.
Case Study Journal
Our record of how the systems of individual buildings were resolved, up to ten entries, written with its engineers.
On the boards
A project in design or permitting. The phrase architects use for work that is drawn but not yet under construction.
