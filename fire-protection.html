What it is

Fire protection is the discipline the building hopes never to use.

Fire protection engineering design covers fire sprinkler systems, standpipes, fire alarm and detection, the water supply and backflow that feed them, the hazard analysis that sets the design criteria and the code compliance that ties them to the architect's egress and the fire marshal's review.

Architects rely on it on restaurants, hotels, retail, offices and apartment buildings. Fire sprinkler and fire alarm contractors work from the criteria, plans and sequences we issue. The authority having jurisdiction reviews it.

What it prevents

Heads that sit where the ceiling design allows.

A fire protection set coordinated early keeps sprinkler heads out of the center of a feature ceiling, keeps the riser room where the architect planned a closet and keeps the fire department connection from arriving on the one elevation the owner cared about. It also keeps the fire marshal's comments from arriving after the permit set is sealed.

Questions

Questions architects ask about fire protection.

The full set is on the Questions page.

Does RCI design fire protection?

Yes. Fire protection is part of our scope on projects such as Elisabetta's and High Dive in West Palm Beach and Carriage House in Palm Beach. Oscar Sebelen, PE, is NICET certified.

What is the difference between RCI's fire protection design and the sprinkler contractor's drawings?

We set the design: the hazard classification, the densities, the zones, the riser, the alarm devices and the sequence. The licensed sprinkler and fire alarm contractors prepare the hydraulic calculations and shop drawings from it. We review them during construction.

Which codes govern fire protection in South Florida?

The Florida Fire Prevention Code, which adopts NFPA 1 and NFPA 101, together with NFPA 13 for sprinklers, NFPA 72 for fire alarm and NFPA 14 for standpipes, reviewed by the city or county fire marshal with its own amendments.

When should fire protection be decided?

With the basis of design. Whether a building is sprinklered changes its construction type, its egress and its water supply, so the decision belongs at schematic design, not at permit.
