What it is

Mechanical design is the air a building breathes.

Mechanical engineering design covers the systems that heat, cool, ventilate and dehumidify a building and move smoke out of it in a fire: air handling units, condensing units and heat pumps, variable refrigerant flow systems, rooftop units, ductwork, diffusers and grilles, kitchen exhaust and make-up air, controls and the load calculations that size all of it.

Architects and developers rely on it for homes, retail stores, restaurants, offices, medical buildings, hotels and apartment buildings. Contractors work from the mechanical sheets and the schedules on them. In South Florida the discipline also carries the humidity of a coastal climate and the wind requirements of the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone.

When we enter

As early as the drawings allow.

What we need to see to start and what gets decided first.

  • Floor plans and reflected ceiling plans

    Where the ceilings drop, where they stay high and where a diffuser can and cannot go.

  • Sections and elevations

    The depth above the ceiling, the roof and the places equipment can be screened.

  • The program

    Occupancy, kitchens, gyms, theaters, server rooms and any space with its own comfort or exhaust need.

  • The site and roof plan

    Where condensing units, rooftop units and outdoor air can sit and how service reaches them.

  • The owner's requirements

    Comfort, noise, brand of equipment, energy targets and what the budget allows.

What we design

The systems, named, with the projects behind them.

  • Load calculations and system selection

    Cooling and heating loads for each space, then the system that fits the architecture, the budget and the schedule, chosen at the basis of design.

  • Variable refrigerant flow

    Refrigerant in small pipes instead of long runs of large duct. Residences in Naples and Tallahassee and the Carriage House restaurant and apartments in Palm Beach.

  • Split, packaged and rooftop systems

    From a single condensing unit behind a house to the equipment on a 93,330 sq ft office headquarters with a six-level garage in Hialeah.

  • Ductwork, diffusers and grilles

    Sized, routed and coordinated with the reflected ceiling plan. On an apartment renovation in Miami Beach, linear slot diffusers with their borders and colors set with the architect.

  • Kitchen exhaust and make-up air

    Hoods, exhaust and the air that replaces it. A food hall in Miami with 12 or more restaurants and a bar, a marina kitchen in Stuart, restaurants from Miami Beach to West Palm Beach.

  • Ventilation and indoor air

    Outdoor air for each occupancy, with the dehumidification a coastal climate needs.

  • Smoke control

    Where the building and its occupancy require it, designed with the fire protection scope.

  • Existing buildings

What it prevents

Ducts that fit the ceiling the architect drew.

A mechanical set coordinated early keeps a list of things from happening on site: a duct that does not fit the ceiling cavity, a rooftop unit the architect never planned room for, a diffuser layout fighting the reflected ceiling plan, a kitchen hood without the make-up air it needs, equipment exposed to wind without the attachments the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone requires.

Each of those costs less on paper than in the field. That is why we ask for the drawings as early as schematic design.

Questions

Questions architects ask about mechanical.

The full set is on the Questions page.

How does RCI keep the systems from compromising the design?

By deciding with the architect where each system lives before it is drawn. Equipment goes where the design wants it hidden or shown. Ducts follow the ceiling heights the architect set. Diffusers and grilles are placed on the reflected ceiling plan and their finish is coordinated with the architect. Two of our residences, in Naples and Tallahassee, use variable refrigerant flow (VRF) systems, which carry refrigerant in small pipes instead of pushing air through long runs of large duct.

What is a variable refrigerant flow system?

A VRF system moves refrigerant from one outdoor unit to several indoor units through small pipes. Each indoor unit cools or heats its own zone. It needs far less duct than a conventional system, which is why we use it in residences where ceiling space and quiet matter, as in Naples and Tallahassee and in the Carriage House restaurant and apartments in Palm Beach.

How does South Florida's climate change the mechanical design?

Humidity. A coastal building needs dehumidification as much as cooling, so outdoor air is treated before it enters and equipment is sized for latent load, not only temperature. In Miami-Dade and Broward counties, the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone adds requirements for rooftop equipment, curbs and attachments exposed to wind.

Does RCI design kitchen ventilation?

Yes. Kitchen hoods, exhaust and make-up air are part of the mechanical scope on our restaurant projects, from a 3,275 sq ft marina kitchen in Stuart to a food hall in Miami with 12 or more restaurants and a bar.
